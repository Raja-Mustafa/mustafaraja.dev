# Midnight Precision: Homepage Blueprint

## Purpose

The homepage is responsible for earning trust as quickly as possible. It reduces uncertainty, establishes credibility, and gives visitors an obvious path to deeper exploration.

Its primary job is not to tell the entire professional story. It is to establish a credible first impression, present the strongest evidence early, and help each visitor choose the next most relevant source of detail. The experience should feel calm, immediate, and considered from the first screen.

## Homepage Objectives

The homepage must answer these questions in order:

1. **Who is this?** Establish the person and professional identity without ambiguity.
2. **What kind of engineer is he?** Communicate the cross-disciplinary focus and current direction.
3. **Can he actually build?** Present representative evidence of execution early.
4. **Why should I trust him?** Support capability with outcomes, technical decisions, research, progression, and credible artifacts.
5. **Where do I go next?** Offer clear paths to deeper work, a resume, and contact.

The ordering is intentional: orientation comes before evidence, evidence comes before supporting context, and every stage preserves a direct next action.

## Design Philosophy

The homepage should feel:

- Calm.
- Confident.
- Deliberate.
- Technical.
- Premium.
- Human.

It must never feel loud or sales-oriented. The visual language should use deep-charcoal restraint, readable hierarchy, generous whitespace, and evidence-led content to create confidence. Motion, if introduced later, must support continuity and direct response without delaying access to information.

## Information Flow

The homepage follows a single narrative: establish identity, prove capability, explain the working mindset, provide depth, show contribution beyond individual projects, then reduce contact friction.

### 1. Hero

**Purpose:** Establish immediate identity and orient visitors to the engineering focus.

**Visitor should understand:**

- Name.
- Engineering focus.
- Current direction.

**Primary question answered:** Who is this, and what kind of work does he do?

**Desired visitor feeling:** Clarity, confidence, and enough curiosity to continue.

**Recommended content:** A concise identity statement, a high-level description of the engineering focus, and a current professional or research direction. Include direct paths to projects and resume material.

**Primary paths:** View Projects and Resume.

**What not to include:** A long biography, exhaustive skill lists, decorative technical graphics, generic mission statements, testimonials, or claims without evidence.

### 2. Featured Work

**Purpose:** Prove capability immediately through the strongest, most representative work.

**Primary question answered:** Can he actually build?

**Desired visitor feeling:** Trust, technical interest, and confidence in the depth behind the portfolio.

**Recommended content:** A selective set of project previews drawn from representative areas such as Software Engineering, Embedded Systems, Semiconductor Research, and Robotics. Each preview should communicate the problem or context, the nature of the contribution, and a concrete outcome or technical decision. Every item should encourage deeper exploration of the project.

**Selection rule:** Only the strongest work belongs here. Choose work that best demonstrates execution, judgment, technical range, and evidence. A smaller set of strong projects is preferable to broad coverage.

**What not to include:** Technology logo walls, equal treatment for all projects, vague summaries, unfinished work without useful context, decorative metrics, or carousel-based browsing.

### 3. Engineering Philosophy

**Purpose:** Communicate how engineering decisions are made.

This is a mindset section, not an About section. It should give visitors a concise view of the principles that connect work across domains: evidence-led reasoning, attention to constraints, systems thinking, craft, and a bias toward useful outcomes.

**Primary question answered:** How does he approach difficult engineering work?

**Desired visitor feeling:** Confidence in judgment and curiosity about the reasoning behind the work.

**Recommended content:** A small number of decision-making principles supported by a brief connection to real work or recurring practices. The emphasis should remain on how decisions are evaluated, not on personality claims.

**What not to include:** A personal history, motivational statements, abstract values without examples, long-form autobiography, or self-promotional language.

### 4. Experience Highlights

**Purpose:** Show progression through the most meaningful experiences.

This section is not a chronological record. It should identify the experiences that most clearly demonstrate increasing responsibility, stronger technical judgment, collaboration, and practical impact.

**Primary question answered:** What sustained contexts demonstrate professional growth and contribution?

**Desired visitor feeling:** Confidence that the portfolio represents real progression rather than isolated work.

**Recommended content:** Selected roles, labs, teams, or initiatives with concise context, the nature of the responsibility, and an outcome or learning signal. Connect each highlight to the broader engineering narrative.

**What not to include:** Full resume duplication, timeline padding, role descriptions without contribution, every short-term activity, or titles treated as evidence by themselves.

### 5. Research and Publications

**Purpose:** Demonstrate technical depth, rigor, and a research-minded approach.

**Primary question answered:** Is there evidence of careful investigation and technical depth?

**Desired visitor feeling:** Respect for rigor and interest in exploring the underlying work.

**Recommended content:** A concise selection of research projects, publications, posters, reports, experimental work, or technical artifacts. Emphasize the question, method, result, and relevance. Provide clear paths to inspect the source material where it is public and appropriate.

**What not to include:** Dense abstracts, unannotated figures, every publication treated equally, inaccessible technical jargon, or research claims without context.

### 6. Leadership and Community

**Purpose:** Show leadership through action, not title.

**Primary question answered:** How does he contribute beyond individual technical tasks?

**Desired visitor feeling:** Confidence in collaboration, initiative, and positive practical influence.

**Recommended content:** Concrete examples of mentorship, team ownership, workshops, public speaking, organizing, community contribution, or initiatives that produced a meaningful outcome. State what was done and what changed.

**What not to include:** Title lists, generic soft-skill claims, honorary labels without evidence, or leadership narratives disconnected from action.

### 7. Contact

**Purpose:** Reduce friction and invite a relevant conversation.

**Primary question answered:** How do I take the next step?

**Desired visitor feeling:** Confidence that reaching out is straightforward and welcome.

**Recommended content:** Direct, professional ways to connect, along with contextual routes to a resume and deeper work. The invitation should be clear without pressuring the visitor.

**What not to include:** Long forms, unnecessary qualification steps, forced account creation, social links without context, or repeated calls to action that compete with the portfolio evidence.

## Section Principles

Every homepage section must follow these operating rules:

- **Purpose:** State one clear reason the section exists within the homepage narrative.
- **Primary question answered:** Resolve a specific visitor uncertainty rather than repeat information from another section.
- **Desired visitor feeling:** Support calm, curiosity, trust, precision, craftsmanship, or a clear next-step confidence.
- **Recommended content:** Use concise, evidence-led material that belongs at homepage depth and links to deeper context when needed.
- **What not to include:** Exclude content that dilutes the section's purpose, duplicates evidence, delays scanning, or creates visual clutter.

No section should require a visitor to read every preceding section to make sense of it. At the same time, the combined sequence should become progressively more convincing.

## Navigation Strategy

Navigation should be persistent, minimal, and immediately understandable. It is a utility for orientation and next steps, not a showcase of interaction design.

- Maintain a persistent header that gives access to the primary destinations without obscuring content or reading flow.
- Limit navigation to the most important destinations, organized around how visitors evaluate the portfolio: work, experience or research where appropriate, resume, and contact.
- Clearly indicate the current page or location using established semantic color, border, or typographic hierarchy. Do not rely on motion or color alone.
- Keep the resume easy to reach from the header and from the hero path.
- Keep contact easy to reach from the header and from the closing section.
- Ensure navigation remains fully usable by keyboard, at narrow widths, and when text is enlarged.

## Scanning Strategy

The homepage must work at three levels of attention.

### Five-Second Scan

Visitors should identify the name, engineering focus, and immediate paths to projects and resume. Clear primary hierarchy, concise labels, and strong visual grouping make this possible without reading long text.

### Thirty-Second Scan

Visitors should recognize representative work, the major areas of expertise, and at least one credible sign of execution. Descriptive section headings, short project summaries, visible outcomes, and deliberate whitespace should make the central narrative apparent.

### Deep Reading

Visitors who continue should find progressively richer evidence: project context, engineering decisions, experience highlights, research rigor, and leadership outcomes. Structured headings, predictable section rhythm, concise metadata, and links to detailed material allow depth without making the homepage dense.

At all levels, headings establish the narrative, spacing separates ideas, summaries provide context, and evidence carries the persuasive weight. The visual system should make it easy to stop and resume without losing orientation.

## Homepage Rules

The following are explicitly prohibited:

- Skill bars.
- Technology logo walls.
- Long About sections.
- Carousels.
- Auto-playing videos.
- Testimonials above evidence.
- Stock photography.
- Large decorative graphics.
- Meaningless statistics.

Also avoid lengthy introductions, navigation that hides primary paths, visual effects that compete with reading, repeated calls to action, and any homepage element that asks visitors to trust a claim before showing its proof.

## Success Criteria

The homepage succeeds if a recruiter can answer the following within one minute:

- Who is this?
- What can he build?
- Why should I trust him?
- How do I learn more?

It also succeeds when a technical visitor can quickly locate deeper evidence, and when a visitor unfamiliar with the domain can still understand the professional identity, quality of work, and next available action.

## Closing Principle

"Attention is borrowed. Trust is earned."

— Midnight Precision
